Spike S4 (system design, section 12) asks how long the coursework model's 31 GB of weights take to
load from S3 with the loader tuned. AWS measured 15 GB in about 5 seconds and 200 GB in about 25
seconds with the Run:ai streamer tuned, but defaults can be ten times slower, one model was
reported slower than the plain loader, and those timings come from an instance with far more
network bandwidth than a single-GPU type has (sections 4.4 and 5.2; evidence E11). The cold-start
budget estimates 20 to 100 seconds for this stage (section 5.2).

## The pass condition

Under 100 seconds for the coursework model's 31 GB. If it fails, the pool's loader becomes
download, then load: the pool's `loader`, `stream` or `download`.

## No job

The spike has no job of its own, and no build starts it. It is read as S1 is
([`s1.md`](s1.md)): every launch records what each of its stages took (section 7.2), and the spike
is read from the weights stage of the launches made under each loader setting. That stage also
holds the engine's own initialization, which the lifecycle agent cannot tell apart from the load
(`services/engine/lifecycle/rhi_lifecycle/stages.py`).

## The reading

No reading yet, so the verdict is pending, and the record has no run and no row. The reading, the
derived numbers and the verdict against the pass condition, is written from a real run's data
(section 12), once sessions have recorded launches under the loader settings to compare. Recorded
launches need the coursework pool on its GPU (needs gpu:capacity), and are the owner's.
