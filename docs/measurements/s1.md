Spike S1 (system design, section 12) asks, on the coursework pool's instance type: how long is each
stage of a launch, from launch to serving, and how long did each instance wait for capacity before
it? Every start here is the launch of a new instance (section 5.2). Stock setups measured 10 to 13
minutes; the budget of section 5.2 estimates about 3 to 13 minutes for a launch here, from
published stage timings. No request waits for a launch (section 5.3). The measured launch sets how
far ahead of a class a session must be booked, the timeout of the launch hook (section 4.3) and
what each start costs in GPU-minutes before the instance serves.

## The pass condition

Launch to serving under 13 minutes at the 90th percentile over 20 launches. If it fails: bake the
engine image into the machine image, and give the launch hook a longer timeout.

## No job

The spike has no job of its own, and no build starts it. Every launch records what each of its
stages took: the lifecycle agent writes the seconds per stage of each launch, and the node's
collector sends them to Prometheus (section 7.2). The spike is read from those records, over the
launches that sessions, the verification and the canary make.

## The reading

No reading yet, so the verdict is pending, and the record has no run and no row. The reading, the
derived numbers and the verdict against the pass condition, is written from a real run's data
(section 12), once sessions have recorded launches: a reading written before any run guesses at
data nobody has seen. Recorded launches need the coursework pool on its GPU (needs gpu:capacity),
and are the owner's.
